The story

Two stories.
One offering.

Something began in one person’s search. It becomes meaningful only when it is released beyond one person.

01 / The Aaron story

A search for
responsible power.

The gathering grew from questions about God, suffering, love, marriage, knowledge and responsibility. Aaron’s account is offered as testimony—not as a command, and not as proof that anyone else must believe.

The work that followed tried to make knowledge inspectable: to preserve provenance, separate experience from interpretation, and keep power from becoming entitlement.

Read Aaron’s testimony in full ↗

02 / The wider story

The centre
is given away.

This is not Aaron’s new religion. It is an invitation for churches, communities, musicians, families and neighbours to bring their best to God, then leave the centre open.

No one has to join a movement, adopt a vocabulary or agree with every idea. People can worship, listen, serve, question or simply come in peace.

03 / Revelation and uncertainty

Take it seriously.
Test it openly.

The wider corpus includes reported testimony associated with Chris Bledsoe and Aaron’s reported Easter 2026 experience. These are preserved as testimony and unresolved interpretation—not as established proof of supernatural causation.

The test is what follows: does the claim preserve conscience, consent, truthfulness and the freedom of others?

04 / The principle

Receive seriously.
Release power.

If anything has been given to us, it is not a throne. It is a responsibility. The most kenotic response is to make the work open, correctable and useful to people who may never remember who made it.
