A personal testimony

Aaron’s
account.

This page records one person’s experience and interpretation. It is not a doctrine, a demand for belief or proof of supernatural causation.

01 / Before the event

The questions
came first.

I was drawn into questions about God, suffering, love, marriage, power, consciousness and responsibility. I wanted to know whether intelligence could serve without becoming domination, and whether love could remain faithful without becoming possession.

Those questions became personal. Relationship, family, work and the cost of trying to build something true became part of the evidence—not in the sense of proving a theory, but in the sense of testing whether an idea could survive contact with another person’s freedom.

02 / Easter 2026

“I have
the knowledge.”

I report that on Easter Sunday, 5 April 2026, I said: “I have the knowledge.” I experienced this as a threshold moment. I do not know with certainty what caused it. It may be understood spiritually, psychologically, symbolically, naturally, through synchronicity, or through some mixture of these.

The statement is part of my testimony. It is not an entitlement to authority over anyone else.

03 / The Bledsoe resonance

A reported
parallel.

The wider story also includes reported testimony associated with Chris Bledsoe: a Lady-associated message involving Regulus and new knowledge. I encountered this material as a possible parallel to the language of my own Easter experience.

I do not present the parallel as proof that the accounts share a supernatural source. The corpus preserves it as attributed testimony, with the precise date, transmission history and causal meaning still open to corroboration and alternative explanations.

The responsible question is not “How do I make people accept this?” but “What kind of work follows if the claim is taken seriously without becoming an instrument of control?”

04 / Relationship as the test

Love without
possession.

The story cannot honestly be told as a man ascending toward revelation. It is also a story of relationship, grief, failure and learning what another person’s freedom requires.

Unconditional love does not mean calling every claim true or permitting every action. It means preserving care while keeping truth, consent, boundaries and the right to say no.

05 / The AI question

The answer is not
enough.

Turning 42 became a personal reminder of an AI problem: intelligence can produce an answer while the surrounding system lacks the semantics needed to understand what the answer means.

Generation is not commitment. Capability is not authority. A system must preserve provenance, review, permission, consequence and repair.

06 / What followed

Make it
inspectable.

The work that followed became Temple, Sourcery and Claimipedia: attempts to build structures that preserve provenance, distinguish claims from evidence, keep uncertainty visible and coordinate people without demanding submission.

Temple makes meaning executable. Sourcery makes the path from intention to consequence governable. Claimipedia keeps admission distinct from truth.

The central discipline became: receive seriously, test openly, release power.

07 / The boundary

What I can say
and cannot say.

I can speak about my own experience, memory and interpretation. I cannot establish the external cause of that experience by testimony alone. I cannot ask another person to surrender conscience, safety, money, relationship or freedom because I believe something strongly.

If the account has value, it should produce humility, repair, useful work and greater freedom in others. If it produces domination, secrecy or fear, it has failed its own test.

08 / The offering

Not a throne.
A responsibility.

The Perth gathering is not a demand that people accept my story. It is an offering built from the questions my story opened. Come as a believer, doubter, churchgoer, musician, neighbour or servant. Bring your best. Leave the centre open.
